## 5.2

No. The current project outline does not specify any qualitative or quantitative metrics or methodologies for measuring the fairness of the system's outcomes. Since the tool uses autonomous agents to synthesize large datasets and generate policy recommendations, there is an inherent risk of amplifying historical biases. To address this, the team should establish clear and measurable acceptance criteria and leverage standard bias and fairness audit toolkits (such as Aequitas) or specify custom metrics to measure and mitigate identified harms throughout the system's lifecycle (NAIC RAI Tools, p.20; VAISS, s 10).

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.20], [VAISS, s 10]*

## 8.1

No. The current project outline identifies primary users (policy analysts and data officers) and stakeholders (department heads and the general public), but it does not indicate that any consultation has actually taken place. Stakeholder engagement is critical, particularly at the earliest stages of the AI lifecycle, to identify potential harms, understand unintended consequences, and evaluate the needs of potentially impacted groups, including marginalized communities (VAISS, s 10).

*Citations: [VAISS, s 10]*

## 8.2

No. The current project outline does not state whether any information about the Agentic Data Synthesis Tool will be made publicly available. For public sector AI systems that influence policy decisions affecting the general public, publishing details about the system's purpose, scope, limitations, and risk mitigation measures is essential to establish legitimacy, public trust, and social licence. This can be achieved by publishing algorithmic impact assessments or maintaining a public AI system register (NAIC RAI Tools, p.34; VAISS, s 10).

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.34], [VAISS, s 10]*

## 8.4

No. The current project outline does not specify whether users or affected stakeholders will be informed when they interact with the AI system or receive AI-generated outputs (8.4.1), nor does it state whether members of the public will have the ability to request a non-AI alternative (8.4.2). Under responsible AI standards, organisations should disclose when AI is used, its role, and when content is AI-generated to build trust and prevent misuse of outputs, especially since generative models can hallucinate facts. Furthermore, business processes should include clear information access, complaint pathways, and human-in-the-loop alternatives where appropriate (NAIC RAI Tools, p.34; VAISS, s 10; ADM Guide (earlier ed.), p.30).

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.34], [VAISS, s 10], [ADM Guide (earlier ed.), p.30]*

## 8.5

Because the tool uses autonomous agents to perform iterative analysis and synthesize findings, providing clear, understandable, and reviewable explanations of how the system arrived at its outputs is critical for maintaining accountability and enabling human oversight (VAISS, s 10; ADM Guide (earlier ed.), p.30).

*Citations: [VAISS, s 10], [ADM Guide (earlier ed.), p.30]*
