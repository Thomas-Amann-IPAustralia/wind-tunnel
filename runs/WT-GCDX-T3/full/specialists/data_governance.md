## 6.1

Yes. The Policy Impact Mapper uses public legislation and a curated, version-controlled snapshot of internal agency documents, which are highly suitable for mapping legislative dependencies. The suitability and quality of this data are ensured through several mechanisms aligned with the National AI Centre's Data Quality Checklist:

1. **Accuracy and Consistency**: The system operates on a curated, version-controlled snapshot of internal documents and public legislation, verifying against trusted sources to prevent incorrect mappings (Data Quality Checklist, Accuracy).
2. **Timeliness**: The system relies on metadata (owner, status, version, dates) to ensure it operates on the current approved state of agency documentation (Data Quality Checklist, Timeliness).
3. **Uniqueness**: The tool is designed to flag version conflicts or duplicates for human resolution rather than guessing, preventing skewed results from redundant or outdated files (Data Quality Checklist, Uniqueness).
4. **Traceability (Lineage)**: It maintains an audit trail of which specific document versions were used for each analysis, ensuring that users can prove which data source influenced a specific AI output (Data Quality Checklist, Traceability (lineage)).
5. **Calibration and Testing**: For the Proof of Concept (POC), the system uses a frozen document set and is seeded with a 'gold standard' set of 10-15 historical changes to calibrate retrieval rules, glossary usage, and confidence thresholds before live testing, ensuring the data coverage is validated against known scenarios (Data Quality Checklist, Data coverage/bias).

Not applicable. Based on the use-case outline, the Policy Impact Mapper is designed to ingest public legislation and a curated snapshot of internal agency documents to map legislative dependencies for internal policy, legal, and compliance staff. It does not specifically collect, use, or target Indigenous data, nor do its primary outputs relate directly to Indigenous people. However, if the agency's internal policies or the legislation being mapped contains Indigenous data or directly impacts Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander communities, the agency must partner with Indigenous stakeholders and embed co-design in its data governance activities to ensure consistency with the Framework for the Governance of Indigenous Data (DDGS, p.13).

## 8.3

Yes. The agency is establishing processes to maintain appropriate documentation and records throughout the lifecycle of the Policy Impact Mapper, in compliance with the Archives Act 1983 and National Archives of Australia (NAA) guidance:

1. **System-Generated Records**: The tool automatically maintains an audit trail of which specific document versions were used for each analysis (traceability). To meet Commonwealth recordkeeping obligations, the AI-generated outputs (the generated impact maps, recommendations, and the user's decisions to accept, reject, or assign them) and associated metadata must be captured and retained in the agency's approved records management system (EDRMS), as AI systems themselves generally lack adequate records management functionality (NAA AI Records Guidance, §General principles for managing records generated by AI; NAA AI Records Guidance, §Example: AI technologies incorporated into a regulatory compliance assessment business system).
2. **System Inputs and Source Data**: The agency will retain the inputs and source data for the assessments, including the uploaded legislation and the specific version-controlled snapshots of internal documents used, to preserve adequate evidence of how business decisions and policy mappings were achieved (NAA AI Records Guidance, §Example: AI technologies incorporated into a regulatory compliance assessment business system).
3. **Development and Governance Records**: Records documenting the development, calibration (including the 'gold standard' set of 10-15 historical changes, retrieval rules, glossary, and confidence thresholds), testing, and governance of the AI system constitute Commonwealth records and will be captured and managed in accordance with the Archives Act 1983, generally covered under AFDA Express Version 2 (Archives Act 1983, s 24; NAA AI Records Guidance, §Records supporting development and implementation of AI systems).

*Citations: [NAA AI Records Guidance, §General principles for managing records generated by AI], [NAA AI Records Guidance, §Example: AI technologies incorporated into a regulatory compliance assessment business system], [NAA AI Records Guidance, §Records supporting development and implementation of AI systems], [Archives Act 1983, s 24]*
