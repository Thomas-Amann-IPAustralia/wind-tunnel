## 5.1

No. While the Policy Impact Mapper is designed as a decision-support tool to map legislative changes to internal policies, the project does not currently have an explicitly documented definition of what constitutes a fair process or outcome. To ensure fairness, the system owners should establish a clear definition that prevents the tool from systematically overlooking or misinterpreting legislative changes that remove discriminatory barriers or introduce new protections, particularly for marginalised groups.

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.19]*

## 5.2

No. The project's current success criteria and metrics focus on general system performance and accuracy (such as the ratio of confirmed vs. rejected suggestions, false positive categorization, and regression testing against a gold standard set). It does not have a quantitative or qualitative method to measure the fairness of outcomes, such as evaluating whether retrieval errors or false negatives disproportionately affect policies related to specific demographic groups or protected attributes.

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.20]*

## 8.1

*Citations: [VAISS, s 10]*

## 8.2

No. Currently, there are no plans to make information about this internal decision-support tool publicly available, as it operates strictly on unclassified internal agency documentation. However, to build public confidence and align with transparency principles, the agency should consider publishing high-level information about the tool's scope, goals, and human-in-the-loop safeguards on a public AI register.

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.34]*

## 8.4

No.
- 8.4.1: Internal staff (policy officers) will be fully informed when interacting with the system via the web-based dashboard. However, members of the public do not interact with the AI system directly, nor do they receive AI-generated outputs, as the tool is strictly an internal decision-support system for mapping policy dependencies.
- 8.4.2: Because the public does not interact with the system, providing them with a non-AI alternative is not applicable. For internal staff, manual policy review remains the standard alternative if they choose not to use the tool.

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.33]*

## 8.5

Yes. The Policy Impact Mapper is designed to provide clear citations and maintain an audit trail of the specific document versions used for each analysis. This allows policy officers to trace and understand the factors (i.e., the semantic relationships and specific text segments) that led to the system flagging a particular policy dependency, supporting effective human review and mitigating automation bias.

*Citations: [NAIC RAI Tools, p.35]*

## 10.1

No. While the project successfully incorporates a multidisciplinary team with diverse professional skills (including policy, legal, compliance, and operational staff), it has not yet established a formal process to ensure demographic diversity or the inclusion of individuals with diverse backgrounds, experiences, and accessibility expertise within the project lifecycle. Incorporating these perspectives is critical to identifying potential blind spots in how legislative changes affecting marginalised groups are retrieved and mapped.

*Citations: [Diversity & Inclusion in AI, p.8], [NAIC RAI Tools, p.14]*
