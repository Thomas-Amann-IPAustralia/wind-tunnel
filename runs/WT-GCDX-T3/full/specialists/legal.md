## 9.1

Not applicable. The Policy Impact Mapper is an internal decision-support tool used exclusively by policy, legal, and compliance staff to identify dependencies between new legislation and internal documents (Outline, Section 3). It does not make administrative decisions or take administrative actions that have a direct legal or significant effect on individuals, groups, or communities. Any subsequent administrative actions or policy updates are reviewed, approved, and executed by human officers (Outline, Section 8). Therefore, direct notification to external parties regarding the use of this internal tool is not applicable, in line with the transparency principles which focus on systems that significantly impact or engage directly with people.

*Citations: [ADM Better Practice Guide, p.10]*

## 9.2

Not applicable. As discussed in section 9.1, the AI system is an internal decision-support tool and does not make or directly influence individual administrative actions affecting external parties (Outline, Section 8). Consequently, there are no AI-influenced administrative actions that require a specific challenge process under this section. External stakeholders retain their standard administrative law rights to challenge any final human-made decisions or policies through existing agency review channels, the Commonwealth Ombudsman, or judicial review.

*Citations: [ADM Better Practice Guide, p.10], [New Machinery of Government, p.17]*

## 10.2

Yes. The agency is ensuring alignment with human rights obligations by engaging a multidisciplinary project team—comprising policy, legal, compliance, and operational staff—to oversee the design, testing, and implementation of the POC (Outline, Section 3). This multidisciplinary approach aligns with best practice recommendations for automated and decision-support systems. As the project progresses beyond the POC stage, formal legal reviews will be conducted to verify that the tool's retrieval logic and the resulting policy updates continue to uphold and align with human rights and administrative law obligations.

*Citations: [ADM Better Practice Guide, p.13], [ADM Better Practice Guide, p.49]*

## 11.1

Yes. Responsibility and accountability are established through compliance with the Policy for the Responsible Use of AI in Government, which requires the designation of an accountable official and an accountable use case owner, as well as registration of the tool on the agency's internal AI use case register. At the system level, accountability is supported by a mandatory human-in-the-loop workflow where policy officers must manually review, confirm, or reject all AI-generated suggestions before any actions are assigned (Outline, Sections 5, 8). Additionally, the system maintains a robust audit trail of the specific document versions used for each analysis to prevent version conflicts and ensure traceability.

*Citations: [AI Use Policy v2.0, p.11], [ADM Better Practice Guide, p.57]*
