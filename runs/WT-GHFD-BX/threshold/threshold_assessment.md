### 3.1 Reducing service accessibility and inclusion

The toolkit is an internal administrative tool assisting data stewards. However, if the AI misclassifies datasets, it could lead to incorrect access restrictions or premature deletion of data, creating short-term barriers to information access. Since the outline does not detail mitigation or accuracy rates, a precautionary 'Possible' likelihood and 'Minor' consequence are adopted.

*Divergence: Assessor A suggested a Minor consequence, while Assessor B suggested Insignificant. The higher tier (Minor) stands because misclassification could lead to incorrect access restrictions or premature deletion of data, creating short-term barriers to information access.*

### 3.2 Unfair discrimination

The toolkit processes datasets containing PII. If the AI model contains underlying biases, it may inconsistently classify or apply retention policies to datasets associated with specific demographic groups or vulnerable communities, leading to noticeable differences in how their data is governed. In the absence of details on bias testing, this is precautionarily rated as 'Possible' with a 'Moderate' consequence.

### 3.3 Stereotyping or demeaning representations

While the tool is an internal backend classifier, there is a minor risk that unstructured documents containing sensitive cultural or demographic information could be mislabeled or grouped under biased category tags generated by the AI. This could potentially reinforce stereotypes for internal data stewards. A precautionary likelihood of 'Possible' is applied due to the lack of detail on the classification criteria.

### 3.4 Harm

The tool suggests and automates policy enforcement, such as retention policies. If the AI incorrectly flags critical datasets for deletion or exposes sensitive PII due to misclassification, it could cause noticeable distress, operational harm, or compliance failures for individuals and the organization. Given the lack of detail on safeguards, a precautionary likelihood of 'Possible' is assumed.

Implementing an AI toolkit that integrates deeply with internal databases and unstructured document repositories introduces potential system vulnerabilities (e.g., prompt injection if using LLMs, insecure API integrations, or data pipeline leaks). A precautionary likelihood of 'Possible' is applied.

## 4. Threshold assessment outcome

### 4.1 Assessing officer recommendation
The proposed Automated Data Governance Toolkit aims to address critical inefficiencies in manual data classification and policy enforcement. However, because the toolkit is designed to scan and process internal datasets that likely contain personally identifiable information (PII) and sensitive government data, it introduces notable risks regarding privacy, data security, and potential classification bias. Any classification errors could lead to unauthorized data exposure or the premature deletion of critical records under automated retention policies.

To ensure safe and compliant implementation, it is recommended that the project team establish strict human-in-the-loop validation protocols, ensuring that the AI only acts as an assistant and does not execute policy enforcement without human sign-off. Furthermore, comprehensive security and privacy impact assessments should be conducted to ensure that the integration of this toolkit with internal databases does not introduce new system vulnerabilities. The classification models must be rigorously tested against diverse datasets to prevent systematic misclassification. Continuous monitoring by data stewards will be essential to mitigate risks and maintain public trust.

A full assessment is **required**.

Overall inherent risk is **High** — refer to an internal governance body (§12.5).
