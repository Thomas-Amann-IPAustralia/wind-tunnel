## 6.1

Yes. The chosen data—consisting of internal departmental documents, meeting minutes, project reports, wikis, and email archives—is inherently suitable because it directly represents the internal knowledge, policies, and project history that the Departmental Knowledge Graph is designed to map. However, to ensure the data is suitable and reliable for AI processing, the department must apply the National AI Centre's Data Quality Checklist. This includes verifying accuracy (ensuring data correctly reflects real entities), completeness (confirming all required fields and documents are present), consistency across different systems, timeliness (understanding delays in data availability), and uniqueness (identifying duplicate records to avoid skewing results) (Data Quality Checklist, p.1-2). Additionally, the department must address data coverage and bias to ensure the ingested documents cover all expected scenarios and groups, and establish traceability (lineage) to prove which data sources influenced specific AI-extracted relationships (Data Quality Checklist, p.3).

## 6.2

No. The outline does not state whether the ingested departmental documents and communications contain Indigenous data, or if consistency with the Framework for Governance of Indigenous Data has been ensured. Because the system ingests a wide range of unstructured internal documents, meeting minutes, and email archives, it is highly likely to include data relating to Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people, communities, or policies. To ensure consistency with the Framework, the department must partner with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people at all stages of the data lifecycle to reflect their priorities, build organizational and cultural change to support their inclusion in data governance, and embed co-design in data collection, use, and governance activities (DDGS, p.13). Additionally, potential risks to Indigenous cultural and intellectual property must be managed as part of the department's AI incident and risk management processes (AI Use Policy v2.0, p.20).

*Citations: [DDGS, p.13], [AI Use Policy v2.0, p.20]*

## 8.3

No. The outline does not state whether processes have been established to maintain appropriate documentation and records throughout the lifecycle of the Departmental Knowledge Graph. To comply with the Archives Act 1983, all AI-generated content and related records created or received by the department constitute Commonwealth records and must be actively managed to retain their authenticity, reliability, integrity, and usability (NAA AI Records Guidance, §General principles for managing records generated by AI). The department must establish processes to capture AI-generated outputs (such as the extracted nodes, edges, and relationship maps) and associated metadata into an approved records management system, as the AI system itself is unlikely to have adequate recordkeeping functionality (NAA AI Records Guidance, §General principles for managing records generated by AI; §Managing AI technologies incorporated into business systems). Furthermore, the department must document and retain records of the system's design, development, testing, configuration, and operational specifications (including details of the AI model, rules framework, and training datasets), as well as policies and procedures governing its correct usage (NAA AI Records Guidance, §Managing AI technologies incorporated into business systems; §Records supporting development and implementation of AI systems; §Example: AI technologies incorporated into a regulatory compliance assessment business system).
